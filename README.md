This study quantifies shifts in government sentiment over time to investigate how changes to monthly bilateral sentiment between the United States and China, as measured by the rhetoric used in government texts, affect their relationship, proxied by macroeconomic indicators including the exchange rate (Chinese Yuan per United States Dollar), tariff rate, and trade balance (U.S. exports to China minus Chinese exports to the U.S.). This research emphasizes the importance of analyzing qualitative aspects, specifically rhetorical sentiment, as a means of capturing changes to the diplomatic and economic relationship between the U.S. and China, and thus the potential resulting effects on key economic indicators. Quantifying the change in sentiment allows for utilization of machine learning models that can measure the significance of correlations between sentiment and the macroeconomic variables, and thus how sentiment can both impact and reflect the U.S.-China relationship.

The Natural Language Processing packages Natural Language Toolkit Opinion Lexicon and TextBlob are used to calculate sentiment. A regression-based approach is used to analyze the relationship between shifts in bilateral sentiment and measurable economic indicators, with the goal of identifying empirical evidence of changes in sentiment as a significant factor in economic relationships. Moreover, how sentiment, as a result, provides insight into how international relations are not only shaped by formal agreements or quantifiable measures, but also by choice of communication in official language.
